# 0109: Dates to the end of 2040 for every site (extends ADR 0104)

Status: accepted, 15 November 2026 (night). Adopted by the author after a competing entry was seen to cover 2027 to 2040 while this
tool stopped at the end of 2030.

## Context

ADR 0104 opened every listed site and own coordinates to the end of 2030: the frozen JPL Horizons tables up to 2027-11-01, the
in-browser engine (Astronomy Engine, `src/moonsky.js`) after them, on the same hourly grid and with the same horizon. The engine
label on the cards says "validated against JPL Horizons" only when `data/validation-years.json` passes the rule in
`config/years.json`. Nothing in the engine stops at 2030; the limit was the accepted range and the checked range.

## Decision

- `config/years.json` `stop` = 2041-01-01 00:00 UTC, so all of 31 December 2040, for every listed site and own coordinates. No code
  change: the range, the date fields, "Now" and the engine blocks (`chunk_days` 365, `pad_days` 45) all read the config.
- The check of the later years covers the whole engine range up to the new end: `check.from` 2028-01-01, `check.to` 2041-01-01,
  `check.pairs` 60 (was 24), same fixed seed 20261116, never on a table hour, a fresh one-minute JPL Horizons request per pair and
  body with the script's pause between requests (`config/validation.json` `pause_seconds`). Rule unchanged: every difference at most
  `max_diff_deg` (0.05°) and every yes/no answer the same, otherwise the cards say the later years are not validated.
- Tests: the accepted range ends 2041-01-01; the check reaches that end; the recorded run has at least 20 pairs in 2031 to 2040;
  the page and the README quote the file's own numbers and years.
- The at-scale check (ADR 0108) is not widened (2026 to 2030); its `all_checks` total is recomputed from the cached answers so it
  counts the new later-year pairs.

## Result of the run (15 November 2026, 22:36 MYT, real JPL Horizons API)

- 60 pairs, 51 of them in 2031 to 2040, every year 2028 to 2040 present. Same solar power answer 60 of 60, same Earth answer
  60 of 60. Every elevation difference at most 0.0137°.
- Largest difference 5.4775°: the Sun azimuth at Apollo 11, 2028-02-08 14:14 UT, with the Sun 0.56° from the zenith. The engine at
  that exact minute is 0.0006° from Horizons in elevation and 0.0014° on the sky; the error comes from the page joining hourly rows
  with straight lines in azimuth and elevation, which near the zenith moves the shown Sun 0.056° on the sky. The same happens, much
  smaller, with the Sun 85° to 88° below the horizon (0.084° in azimuth, 2036 and 2037).
- The rule is not met, so `passed` is false and the cards say the dates after the precomputed tables are not validated. The
  first-screen total (`all_checks`, 900 pairs) now shows a largest difference of 5.4775°.
- Not done here, needs the author's decision: interpolate the direction (unit vectors) instead of azimuth and elevation, then run
  every Horizons check again. Until then this branch should not be merged over a validated label.

## Rollback

Set `stop`, `check.to` and `check.pairs` back (2031-01-01, 24), run `python3 scripts/validate_years.py` and then
`python3 scripts/validate.py --scale`, and revert the text and test changes of this record.
